    It's best not to touch this. K is the scale constant the formula is built on, and the default of 100 matches the note this is based on. Only change it if you're recalibrating from your own measurements.

    How it works

    A speedflip diagonal is set by the ratio of your roll input to your pitch input. On keyboard, W gives full pitch (1.0). The air roll key gives a smaller value because its Speed= scales it down, and that scaling depends on framerate.

    r = tan(angle) Speed = r · fps / K
    • Deadzone has no effect on these values, so the formula treats it as 0.00.
    • K is 100, based on the note that Speed works as Speed × 100 × frametime. Halving the fps halves the Speed you need.
    • Axis steps: the axis is a signed byte, so only 127 positive (or 128 negative) values exist. With snapping on, the value aims at the middle of the nearest step so rounding and truncation agree.

    Assumptions and limits

    • W has no Speed= of its own, so it stays at full pitch. If your pitch bind also has a Speed, the framerate dependence changes and this formula no longer applies.
    • The framerate must stay steady. A fluctuating fps changes the angle.
    • Angles below about 11.5° probably snap to a straight dodge, so a diagonal may not happen regardless of Speed.
    • K is fitted from one measurement (2.85 at 480 fps gave roughly 30°) plus the quoted formula. To recalibrate, measure the angle you get from a known Speed, then set K = tan(angle) × fps ÷ Speed.
    • Since the amount of steps on each side is slightly different (128/127), you can't get the exact same angle on both sides.
